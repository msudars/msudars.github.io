About

I'm a research scientist at IFE in Norway, where I work out what shape a battery is in from the data it produces while it is being used, and build open-source tools for it. Projects

Open source · IFE

Battlas

A tool for operating telemetry from electric vehicles and battery storage systems. It takes the data a battery produces in normal use and estimates its health, with an interval, so an engineer can decide which cells, modules or systems to look at first. It is developed at IFE within a three-year Research Council of Norway project (FME BATTERY) and builds on cellpy.

Operators of EVs and storage systems already log voltage, current and temperature. What they often lack is a per-unit answer to three questions: which unit is off, how fast is it ageing, and how sure are we? A capacity test means taking the asset out of service, and most estimators give one number without saying when to trust it.

Battlas gives an interval with every estimate, and it declines to answer when a unit's operating conditions are outside what the data covers. It reports exposure, capacity trend, resistance and deviation from sibling units separately instead of folding them into one score, so you can see why a unit moved. It treats lab data as a starting expectation to be updated with each unit's own field data, because models trained in the lab lose accuracy when the duty changes. And it models cells as parts of modules and systems, since field labels usually exist only at module or string level.

It runs on a CPU, on your own machine, so the telemetry stays where it is. It is still in development. If you have telemetry you'd like to try it on, write to me. There is a small interactive demo of the "I don't know" idea.

Research · preprint in preparation

Can AI read battery degradation plots?

I tested seven open vision-language models on diagnostic plots of ageing lithium-ion cells, to see whether they could name the damage. None did better than chance, and all of them sounded confident. There is a short summary and a blog post.

Research

My PhD thesis was Low-Data Machine Learning Framework for Generalizable Battery Degradation and Fault Diagnosis. The main threads were:

  • Forecasting capacity fade from little cycling data, with models small enough to run on a battery management system (DegradAI, npj Clean Energy 2025).
  • Spotting the onset of rapid degradation while a battery is in use (Batteries 2024).
  • Diagnosing faults in EV fleets from manufacturers the model has not seen, with self-supervised pre-training and a few labelled examples.
  • Thermal runaway and abuse: internal temperature measurement and nail puncture (Scientific Reports 2023, Energy 2023).
