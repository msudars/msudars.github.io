A field guide to public battery datasets

Open data limits what battery machine learning can claim: most papers use a single dataset, and field and fault data is scarce. These are the public datasets I have worked with, with the details that are easy to miss. The "watch out for" lines come from using them, not from their documentation.

NASA PCoE Li-ion ageing Lab

The most common benchmark in the SOC and SOH literature.

What it is
18650 cells cycled four at a time, charge, discharge and impedance sweeps (0.1 Hz to 5 kHz), at temperatures from 4 to 44 °C. Delivered as .mat files.
Good for
SOH and SOC benchmarks, impedance-based health work.
Watch out for
Different battery groups use different protocols, end-of-life thresholds and temperatures, so results from one group do not carry to another. Mostly constant loads.
Source
NASA Ames Prognostics Center of Excellence data repository.

NASA randomized battery usage Lab

Cells cycled with random-walk current profiles instead of constant loads, closer to real duty.

What it is
28 LCO 18650 cells (about 2.2 Ah) at 25 and 40 °C, with periodic reference charge and discharge cycles as health benchmarks. Cycled until health fell to between 80% and 50%.
Good for
Testing what happens when duty changes. In my runs, moving from the controlled to the randomized duty tripled the error of a model trained on the controlled sets, and a conformal interval that should cover 90% covered 70%.
Watch out for
Random-walk rests fragment the reference discharges, so a generic detector found only 54% of them. Raw current is discharge-positive.

Sandia National Laboratories 18650 study Lab

A multi-chemistry cycling study in one consistent format.

What it is
18650 cells in LFP, NCA and NMC (LCO appears too), at 15, 25 and 35 °C and discharge rates from 0.5 to 3 C. About 287 MB of spreadsheets.
Good for
Training and testing models that must generalise across chemistry, temperature and rate. It was the main training set for DegradAI.
Watch out for
The test matrix differs by chemistry, for example 3 C discharge for LFP and NMC but not NCA. Some cells needed cleaning before training.
Source
batteryarchive.org

WLTP pack (12s3p NCA) Lab pack

A pack-level dataset where cells share a string.

What it is
36 Panasonic NCR18650B cells wired 12s3p, with 413 per-cycle files and capacity checks per string. Two strings faded to roughly 0.66 and 0.60 health in about five and a half months.
Good for
Learning how series and parallel wiring change what a measurement means.
Watch out for
Capacity labels are per string, not per cell, and in series the string is limited by its weakest cell.
Source
CSUC dataverse.

28 LFP battery systems (LFP-28) Field

Real systems in real use, with per-cell voltage.

What it is
28 portable 24 V LFP systems (about 160 Ah, 8 prismatic cells in series), logged for one month to five years each, 133 million rows from 2013 to 2022. About 5 s sampling.
Good for
Comparing sibling cells within a string, and screening systems without labels.
Watch out for
Every system is a warranty return, so there is no healthy reference population. Only comparisons within a system are well-posed. No capacity labels. About 3% of rows are BMS transients.
Licence
CC-BY-NC-4.0, non-commercial.
Source
Zenodo record 13715694

EVBattery (He et al. 2022) Fleet

Charging data from hundreds of electric vehicles.

What it is
464 EVs from three manufacturers, more than 1.2 million charging snippets of 128 steps, 8 channels each (cell voltage, current, temperature, SOC).
Good for
Fault classification with few labels. In my runs, accuracy (AUROC) rose from 0.58 with 10 labelled vehicles to 0.92 with all 465, still not saturated.
Watch out for
Only a binary fault label per vehicle. The capacity field is zero in every snippet, and there are no absolute timestamps.
Source
arXiv 2201.12358

Tsinghua University EV fault dataset Fleet

A companion fleet dataset with engineer-confirmed faults.

What it is
About 690,000 snippets from 347 EVs of three manufacturers, with current, voltage and temperature.
Good for
Cross-manufacturer fault diagnosis. Pre-training on one manufacturer and testing on the others transferred best from manufacturer B in my thesis work.
Watch out for
It is easy to confuse with EVBattery. They are two different datasets with different sizes and labels.

Where to look for more

  • batteryarchive.org holds data from several labs (CALCE, HNEI, Oxford, Sandia) in one format.
  • Lithium-ion battery data and where to find it (Energy and AI, 2021) reviews 30 datasets.
  • A 2026 conference talk counted about 400 public datasets, and efforts to make them searchable and convert them to a common format are under way.
